# Modernisation style: "light touch"

The modern text is George Long's 1862 translation with its archaic English updated. It is **not** a new translation. A reader comparing the two side by side should see the same sentences, the same order, the same images, and the same ideas.

## The approved example (this is the standard)

> Long: *Begin the morning by saying to thyself, I shall meet with the busy-body, the ungrateful, arrogant, deceitful, envious, unsocial. All these things happen to them by reason of their ignorance of what is good and evil.*
>
> Modern: *Begin the morning by saying to yourself: today I shall meet with the busybody, the ungrateful, the arrogant, the deceitful, the envious, the unsociable. All these things happen to them because they don't know what is good and what is evil.*

Where these rules seem to conflict with the example, match the example's degree of freedom.

## Do

- thou/thee/thy/thine/thyself → you/your/yours/yourself, and fix the verbs to match (art → are, hast → have, wilt → will, dost → do, wouldst → would, -est/-eth endings removed).
- Replace archaic phrasing with its plain modern equivalent, even if that rewords the phrase ("by reason of" → "because"; "art wont to" → "tend to"; "conformable to" → "in keeping with"; "want of" → "lack of"; "hinder" → "stop/prevent" where natural).
- Replace obsolete or uncommon word forms with common ones ("unsocial" → "unsociable", "bloodspitting" → "spitting blood", "approbation" → "approval").
- Add small connective words for flow ("today", a repeated "the"), and split or join a clause with punctuation where it helps.
- Work Long's square-bracket insertions in naturally. If it's a needed word ("not [only]"), just include it. If it's a gloss ("providence or atoms [fortuitous concurrence of things]"), fold it in as a short apposition or drop it if it adds nothing. A modern text must never contain "[" or "]".
- Use British spelling (honour, favour, judgement, vapour, tranquillity).
- Dialogue dashes are a closed em dash: `warm?—But this is more pleasant.—Do you…`
- A few contractions are fine where the example uses them ("don't"), but don't contract everything.
- "daemon" (Marcus's inner divine guide) → "the spirit within" (or "the spirit within a man", following the sentence).

## Don't

- Don't modernise "man/men" into gender-neutral wording. Keep Long's voice.
- Don't change proper nouns or Roman/Greek terms (praetor, Falernian, Clotho, Carnuntum, the Quadi). Add a two- or three-word gloss only if Long's own bracket provides one.
- Don't add ideas, explain, interpret, or resolve an ambiguity Long left open.
- Don't drop clauses, images, or list items. Every list item in Long appears in the modern version.
- Don't change paragraph breaks. The modern text has the same number of paragraphs (`\n\n`) as the original. Place notes like "This in Carnuntum." stay as their own paragraph ("Written at Carnuntum.").
